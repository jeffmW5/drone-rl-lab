[Learning to Fly in Seconds v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.13081v2), IV, combines varied initial states, actuator-state modeling, survival-oriented costs, coordinate shifting and scheduled reward/exploration. V assesses episode length and trajectories because returns depend on the reward definition. This supports behavioral comparisons and recovery tests. Its untethered RPM/TD3/asymmetric actor-critic differs from our privileged full-tether PPO and observed motor-force state; neither its architecture nor published performance proves the proposed reward16. [Author repository](https://github.com/arplaboratory/learning-to-fly), independently verified in0109, was not executed.

[What Matters v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.05990v1),3.2 covers network/output initialization and action scale,3.3 observation/value normalization and clipping,3.4 advantage estimators and value-loss clipping, and3.5 temporal fragments, minibatching and recomputed advantages. These distinctions matter: PPO value/policy clipping is different from Puffer's incoming reward clamp. The paper motivates controlled implementation/scale checks; it does not prescribe our spatial reward or diagnose our failures. The [verified SEED RL repository](https://github.com/google-research/seed_rl) links the study from its configurable-agent README; no code execution.
