[Learning to Fly in Seconds v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.13081v2), IV, trains direct motor policies with actuator dynamics, varied initial states, survival-oriented costs and scheduled reward/exploration. V measures episode behavior alongside returns and repeated training seeds. These methods support assessing trajectory regression, recovery and survival rather than assuming that more training or higher reward means stable control. The paper uses untethered RPM/TD3 and asymmetric observations; our full-tether PPO already observes motor forces. Its published success does not diagnose this simulator's failure or justify a dynamics change. [Verified author code](https://github.com/arplaboratory/learning-to-fly), previously checked in0109, was not executed.

[What Matters v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.05990v1),3.2 studies output initialization and action scale;3.3 treats observation/value normalization and clipping;3.4 addresses advantages and value-loss clipping;3.5 studies data reuse, temporal fragments, minibatches and recomputation. Section3.5 makes total training duration distinct from the temporal credit horizon: adding optimizer steps does not itself lengthen collected fragments. Its findings motivate checking fixed prefixes and exact learner data/updates when a late checkpoint regresses, but do not establish a specific defect in Puffer or a cure for our tether task. [Verified study repository](https://github.com/google-research/seed_rl) links this paper; no author code was executed.
