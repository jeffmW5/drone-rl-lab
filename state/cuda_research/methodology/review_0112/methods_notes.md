## Local implications and counterevidence

Current isotropic speed cost gives the same instantaneous cost to equal-speed motion toward and away from a nonzero position error. Signed position and velocity observations already provide direction; a scalar speed penalty does not imply missing direction information or reversed feedback. A reference-velocity objective could distinguish these directions, but changes the task objective and needs its own falsifiable scalar/trajectory test. At the hover target, zero-reference speed cost is appropriate. None of these papers proves that this reward term explains the actual lateral failures or that replacing it will solve them. Paper action/controller levels also do not permit adding a hidden helper to pure learned mode.
